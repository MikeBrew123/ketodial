Keto Hot Drinks That Aren't Coffee, Ranked by Net Carbs

Chloe · Community Manager · November 20, 2026

Okay so, someone asked me a question this week that I can't stop thinking about. She doesn't drink coffee. She finds tea "only ok." And the keto hot chocolate mix she bought was a letdown. So what does a person actually sip when it's cold out?

Fair question. I live in Whistler, where November means my cat Juno is already in a sweater and I want a hot mug in my hand by mid-afternoon. Coffee can't be the only answer.

Here's the thing. Most people on keto aim for 20 to 30 g of net carbs a day. A mug that costs 3 g is no big deal. A mug that costs 30 g is your whole day. If you don't know your own number yet, the free KetoDial calculator gives you a daily net carb target, so you can see how much room a mug can take.

The Ranking, Lowest Net Carbs First

These are per mug, made at home, using the recipe listed. Change the recipe and the number changes.

  • 1. Plain or herbal tea: about 0 g. Peppermint, rooibos, ginger, chamomile, black, green. Nothing added, nothing to count.
  • 2. Broth or bone broth: about 0 to 1 g per cup. The savoury mug, and the one people who don't like tea tend to fall for.
  • 3. Brewed chai with cream: about 1 g. One chai tea bag, hot water, 2 tablespoons of heavy cream.
  • 4. Golden milk: about 2 g. One cup of unsweetened almond milk, half a teaspoon of ground turmeric, a pinch of cinnamon.
  • 5. Homemade hot chocolate: about 3 g. One cup of unsweetened almond milk, 1 tablespoon of unsweetened cocoa powder, 1 tablespoon of heavy cream, a pinch of salt, and a zero-carb sweetener to taste.

Every mug on that list fits inside a 20 to 30 g day with lots of room left for actual food.

Broth Is the Mug Nobody Thinks Of

If tea is "only ok," stop trying to like tea and go savoury. Hot broth in a mug with a little extra salt feels more like a snack than a drink.

It also does a job. Lower insulin on keto makes the kidneys let go of sodium and water. That's why most people on keto land around 3 to 5 g of sodium a day (about 1.5 to 2 teaspoons of salt), spread through the day. A cup of broth can carry 800 to 1,200 mg of that.

This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

One label note: some boxed broths have added sugar or starch. Read the ingredients and pick one that's basically bones, water and salt. Or make your own. Carnivore Weekly has a good piece on what's in a cup of bone broth and how to get it to gel.

Why Your Hot Chocolate Mix Tasted Thin and Weird

Real talk: it probably wasn't you. Most keto hot chocolate mixes disappoint for the same few reasons, and you can spot them all on the label.

  • Thin cocoa. If cocoa isn't near the top of the ingredient list, the mug will taste like brown water. Rich hot chocolate needs a full tablespoon of real cocoa, and many mixes don't come close.
  • Serving size tricks. The carb count looks great because the serving is tiny. Use enough to actually taste it and you may be drinking two or three servings.
  • Fillers. Maltodextrin and dextrose are sugars by another name. Corn fibre gets used to bulk a mix up, and it can leave a mug oddly slick.
  • "Just add water." Chocolate needs fat to taste like chocolate. Water plus sweetener plus a dusting of cocoa is never going to feel like the real thing.

The fix is boring and it works. Whisk real cocoa into warm almond milk, add cream and a pinch of salt, then sweeten it yourself. The salt is the part people skip, and it's what makes it taste like chocolate instead of sweet dust.

Which sweetener is a personal call. I wrote up my honest take on whether allulose was worth it, and hot drinks are one place it behaves well. Cocoa is also worth adding to your keto staples list, because a tin lasts all winter.

The 8 PM Mug

The after-dinner pantry raid is often a habit more than hunger. Your hands want something to do, and a hot mug takes twenty minutes to finish. That's a lot of minutes you're not standing at the cupboard.

For evenings, go caffeine-free. Coffee after noon is the first thing people cut when sleep is the complaint, and the same logic covers black tea and regular chai. Rooibos, peppermint, chamomile, broth and golden milk are all safe picks. Rooibos chai blends give you the spice without the caffeine.

My vote for 8 pm is the salty one. A small mug of broth is filling in a way a sweet drink isn't, and a small salty drink before bed is a common fix for night cramps and that wide-awake-at-3 feeling. Sarah covers it in her piece on why keto wakes you up at 3 a.m.

Hot chocolate works at night too, but I'll be honest. If a sweet mug makes you want a second sweet thing, it isn't helping. Try the broth for a week and compare.

Does a Creamy Mug "Count"?

For carbs, barely. For calories, yes. A tablespoon of heavy cream is about 50 calories, and a tablespoon of butter is about 100.

That doesn't make cream bad. Most people don't count calories for the first 8 weeks and just eat until they're full. But if your weight stalls for real later on, added fat in drinks is the first thing to trim. Three creamy mugs a day adds up quietly.

What to Order at a Cafe

Skip the chai latte. It's made from a sweetened concentrate plus milk, and it runs 30 to 45 g of carbs. Cafe hot chocolate is syrup and milk, and whole milk alone is about 12 g of carbs per cup.

Here's what works:

  • Any brewed tea or herbal tea, plain
  • A chai tea bag in hot water with a splash of cream, which lands at about 1 g
  • Steamed unsweetened almond milk with cinnamon on top, but ask first, because many cafes only stock a sweetened one

So that's the list. Know your daily number, then pick the mug that fits it. My guess is the broth surprises you.
