PSMF Days: The Fat Loss Lever Nobody Pulls

Marcus · Performance Coach · August 1, 2026

The Deficit Problem Nobody Talks About

Most people run the same deficit every single day. Small, constant, forever. It works until it doesn't, and then they're six months in, down twelve pounds, and half of that came off their legs.

There's a better lever. It's called a protein sparing modified fast, or PSMF, and you pull it one or two days a week instead of grinding a shallow deficit seven days a week.

I've used this with fighters cutting to a weight class and with desk workers who just wanted their jeans to fit. Same tool, different dose. Here's the protocol.

What a PSMF Day Actually Is

Strip a day down to protein and almost nothing else. That's it. Very high protein, minimal fat, near zero carbs. You're eating somewhere between 700 and 1,000 calories, and nearly all of it is protein.

This isn't a new internet trend. Dr. George Blackburn developed the protein sparing modified fast at Harvard in the 1970s for patients who needed rapid fat loss without shredding lean tissue. The protein is the whole point. It's what keeps your body from breaking down muscle to cover the gap.

A normal keto day gets most of its calories from fat. A PSMF day pulls that fat way down so your body has to run on stored fat instead. The protein stays high so your muscle stays put.

The Protein Target

1.2 to 1.5 grams of protein per pound of lean body mass. That's the number. Lean mass, not total body weight.

Run the math. A 190 pound guy at 22% body fat carries about 148 pounds of lean mass. His PSMF protein target is 178 to 222 grams. Call it 200. At 4 calories per gram, that's 800 calories from protein alone.

If you don't know your lean mass, the KetoDial calculator gives you an estimate along with your normal macros. Run your regular numbers first, then use the lean mass figure to build your PSMF target off it. Two different days, two different sets of numbers, and you should know both before you start.

What that looks like on a plate:

  • Chicken breast, 8 oz cooked: 70g protein, 8g fat
  • Egg whites, 1 cup: 26g protein, 0g fat
  • Cod or tilapia, 8 oz: 45g protein, 2g fat
  • Top round or eye of round, 6 oz: 45g protein, 10g fat
  • Plain nonfat Greek yogurt, 1 cup: 22g protein, 0g fat

Notice what's missing. No ribeye, no bacon, no butter, no cheese. Those are great foods on a standard keto day. On a PSMF day they blow your fat budget in one bite.

The Fat Floor (Don't Skip This)

Here's where people get hurt. They read "minimal fat" and take it to zero.

Keep a floor of 20 to 30 grams of fat on PSMF days. Not a target to hit, a minimum to clear.

Two reasons. First, your gallbladder needs dietary fat to contract and move bile. Extended very low fat intake is associated with bile stasis and gallstone formation, which is a documented risk in aggressive weight loss protocols. Second, fat soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K need fat to be absorbed. Zero fat means you're eating vitamins you can't use.

Three whole eggs and a tablespoon of olive oil covers your floor. Done. Stop overthinking it.

Carbs stay under 20 grams, and I'd pull them from spinach, broccoli, or asparagus rather than anything processed. Volume helps when you're hungry.

The Electrolyte Load

This is where PSMF days go sideways, and it's completely preventable.

Insulin is already low on keto. On a PSMF day it drops further, and low insulin tells your kidneys to dump sodium. That's natriuresis, and it takes potassium and water with it. The headache, the dizziness when you stand up, the sudden feeling that this whole thing was a mistake? That's sodium loss, not a character flaw.

Daily targets on a PSMF day:

  • Sodium: 5,000 to 7,000 mg. That's 12 to 18 grams of salt. Higher than your normal keto day, because you're losing more.
  • Potassium: 2,600 mg for women, 3,400 mg for men. Lean meat carries a decent amount. Supplement the gap.
  • Magnesium: 300 to 400 mg. Glycinate or citrate. Take it at night.

I front load sodium in the morning and take a second dose mid afternoon when the crash usually hits. A pre-mixed electrolyte stick works because you don't have to measure anything. Salt in water works too and costs nothing. Pick one and actually do it.

How to Run It

One or two days per week. Non consecutive. Never back to back.

Put them on your lowest activity days. If you lift Monday, Wednesday, Friday, run PSMF Tuesday and Thursday, or Tuesday and Saturday. Light walking is fine. Heavy squats are not.

Every other day, you eat your normal keto macros. Full stop. PSMF is a spike in the deficit, not a new baseline. People who try to run it four or five days a week end up with the exact muscle loss they were trying to avoid, plus a wrecked relationship with food.

Expect the scale to drop 2 to 4 pounds after a PSMF day. Most of that is water and glycogen and it comes back. The real fat loss is roughly half a pound per PSMF day at a 1,500 calorie deficit. Two days a week gets you an extra pound of actual fat per week on top of whatever your baseline deficit is doing. The math doesn't lie, and it doesn't need to be dramatic to work.

Who Should Skip This Entirely

I'm being direct here because this part matters more than the protocol.

  • Anyone on insulin or sulfonylureas. The hypoglycemia risk is real and it's fast.
  • Type 1 diabetics. Not without an endocrinologist actively managing your doses.
  • Anyone with kidney disease. This protein load is not appropriate for compromised kidneys.
  • Any history of disordered eating. Aggressive restriction protocols are a trigger. This one is not worth the risk.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding. No.
  • Already lean. Under 12% body fat for men, under 20% for women, you don't need this lever. You need patience.
  • On a GLP-1 with appetite already suppressed. You're likely under eating protein already. Adding PSMF days compounds the muscle loss problem instead of solving it.

Two out of ten people I've handed this to came back and said it wasn't for them. One couldn't stand the food monotony. One got lightheaded even with electrolytes dialed. Both went back to a steady moderate deficit and did fine. That's a legitimate outcome, not a failure.

Protocol beats willpower. But the right protocol for you beats the aggressive one every time.

Run It for Four Weeks

Pick two days. Hit your protein number. Clear your fat floor. Load your electrolytes. Eat normal keto the other five days.

Track weight weekly, not daily, and track a waist measurement alongside it. If the waist is moving and your lifts are holding, it's working. If your strength is dropping, cut back to one PSMF day or stop.

Measure it, adjust it, repeat it.
