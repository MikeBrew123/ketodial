No Beans, No Potatoes: What Fills the Plate on Keto

Chloe · Community Manager · October 9, 2026

Okay, so here's the moment nobody warns you about. You've sorted out the protein, you know what's for dinner, and then you look at the plate and there's a big empty spot where the potatoes used to go. Or the beans. Or the rice your family still wants.

Real talk: that empty spot is where a lot of people give up on keto. Not because they miss potatoes so much, but because they don't know what goes there instead. So let's fill it.

Why the Potato Spot Matters

On keto, most people aim for 20 to 30 grams of net carbs a day. A plain potato runs about 17 g net carbs per 100 g, and most of us don't stop at 100 g. One normal serving can eat most of your day.

Beans are the same story. They're healthy food for plenty of people, but a half cup of regular black or pinto beans can take a big bite out of a 20 g budget. If you're not sure what your own number is, the KetoDial calculator will give you a daily net carb target, which makes every side dish choice below a lot easier.

The good news? Low-carb vegetables are completely normal on keto. You don't have to eat a plate of plain meat. You just have to swap what's sitting next to it.

The Swaps, Ranked by Net Carbs

All numbers below are net carbs per 100 g (about 3.5 oz), based on USDA-style values for the raw vegetable. Potato is at the bottom for comparison.

  • Radishes: about 2 g
  • Kohlrabi: about 2.6 g
  • Cauliflower: about 3 g
  • Cabbage: about 3 g
  • Green beans: about 4 g
  • Turnips: about 4.6 g
  • Brussels sprouts: about 5 g
  • Celeriac (celery root): about 7 g
  • Black soybeans, canned: roughly 1 to 2 g per 1/2 cup (brand dependent, so check the label)
  • Potato, for comparison: about 17 g

So a generous 200 g scoop of cauliflower mash lands around 6 g net. The same amount of potato is about 34 g. That's the whole trick, right there.

Cauliflower Mash: The One Your Family Might Steal

Everyone talks about cauliflower mash like it's a sad consolation prize. It isn't. Some people find their partner actually prefers it now, which is a pretty big win when you're cooking for a household that still eats potatoes.

Here's the tip that makes it better: use the stems too. Peel them if they're tough, then steam them with the florets. You get more bulk and a thicker mash, and nothing goes in the trash.

Weeknight plate: meatloaf or pork chops, cauliflower mash with a little butter and salt, and gravy over both. Make a small pot of real potatoes for anyone who wants them. Same gravy, everybody's happy.

Roasted Radishes: Yes, Really

This one comes up again and again. Radishes lose their bite when you roast them and turn mild and a little potato-ish. The air fryer is the favorite method, about 15 minutes at 400°F with a light coat of oil, salt, and garlic powder.

Radishes also go surprisingly potato-like in soup. Toss halved ones into a beef or chicken soup and see who notices.

Weeknight plate: roast chicken thighs, air-fried radishes, and a side salad.

Turnips: The Easiest Straight Swap

Turnips are probably the closest thing to a potato on this list. Cube them for a roasting pan, slice them for a gratin, or boil and mash them. They're a little higher in carbs than cauliflower, so keep the portion reasonable.

Weeknight plate: pot roast with turnip chunks right in the pan where the potatoes would go. Add potatoes on one side for the rest of the family.

Kohlrabi: Fried Potatoes, Grown Up

Okay, kohlrabi looks like a tiny alien, but hear me out. Some people slice it thin and pan-fry it to stand in for the fried potatoes they grew up with. Crispy edges, soft middle. If you can't find it at the grocery store, check a farmers market. Some folks even grow their own.

Weeknight plate: eggs, sausage, and pan-fried kohlrabi slices.

Celeriac: Stews, Not a Mountain of Mash

Celery root is great in stews and soups, where it soaks up flavor and holds its shape. But at about 7 g net per 100 g, it's the highest on this list. Use it as one part of a stew, not as a giant pile of mash.

If your store is out of celeriac, a large radish works in a stew too.

Weeknight plate: beef stew with celeriac and radish chunks instead of potatoes.

Black Soybeans: The Bean Answer

People ask about a good bean substitute constantly, and canned black soybeans are the answer a lot of people land on. Even folks who don't love beans say they work. White soybeans are another option. Brands vary a lot, so read the label before you buy.

Weeknight plate: a big pot of chili with ground beef and black soybeans. Serve it with cheese and sour cream, and put out tortilla chips for the kids.

Cabbage, Green Beans, and Brussels Sprouts

These three aren't potato look-alikes, but they fill the plate and they're cheap. Some people swap roasted Brussels sprouts in wherever roasted potatoes used to be. Fried cabbage with a bit of bacon is a classic for a reason, and green beans are the side your family already eats.

Weeknight plates: sausage with fried cabbage, steak with green beans, or salmon with roasted Brussels sprouts.

A Quick Word on Butter

Here's the thing. A pat of butter on your mash is fine and makes it taste good. But for weight loss, protein is the anchor and fat is the lever. You don't need to drown every vegetable in butter or cheese sauce to make keto work. And for your first 8 weeks, don't count calories. Eat until you're satisfied and let your body sort it out.

If you want more swap ideas, our pasta and pizza swaps cover the other big empty spots. For shopping, the keto grocery list has all of these vegetables in one place. And if you're cooking for two with different plates, check out meal prep for couples.

The potato spot doesn't have to stay empty. Pick two swaps this week, try them, and keep the one your family fights over.
