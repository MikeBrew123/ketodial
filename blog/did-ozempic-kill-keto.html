Did Ozempic Kill Keto? The Community Isn't Sure

Chloe · Community Manager · July 21, 2026

Okay, so is keto dead or not?

If you've spent any time in keto forums lately, you've seen the panic. Somebody posts a screenshot of the new dietary guidelines, somebody else drops an Ozempic headline, and suddenly half the thread is asking if we all wasted the last five years counting macros.

Real talk: the vibe is weird right now. GLP-1 drugs are everywhere, the news keeps saying "just take the shot," and keto got a lukewarm shrug from the latest guidelines. So people are nervous. I get it.

But here's the thing. When I actually read what folks are saying instead of the headlines, the story is way more interesting than "keto is over." Let's get into it.

Where the "keto is dead" idea came from

Two things happened at once, and they collided.

First, the GLP-1 wave. Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound, all of it. These drugs work for a lot of people, and the results are hard to argue with. When your neighbor drops 40 pounds without white-knuckling every meal, it makes any diet look slow by comparison.

Second, the newest round of dietary guidance leaned back toward "everything in moderation, watch your saturated fat." That's basically the opposite of how most of us eat. So the keto crowd read it as a cold shoulder.

Put those together and you get the doom posts. "Why bother with keto when there's a shot?" I saw that exact question get 200 comments in a day.

What the community's actually saying

Here's what surprised me. Almost nobody in these threads is quitting keto. They're just arguing about what to do next.

I keep seeing three camps show up over and over:

  • The stackers. These are people on a GLP-1 who eat keto on top of it. Their logic is simple: the drug kills the appetite, and keto tells them what to eat when they do.
  • The "keto instead" crowd. Folks who tried the meds, didn't like the side effects or the cost, and came back to food as their tool.
  • The nervous middle. People who haven't picked a side and just want to know if they're doing something pointless. That's most of the thread, honestly.

What I don't see much of is people trashing each other. A year ago the diet wars were brutal. Now there's this quiet truce where everyone kind of admits obesity is hard and different tools help different people. I'll take it.

The stacking thing is bigger than I expected

I was talking to someone in a keto group who's been on Zepbound for a few months. She said the drug turned the food noise off, but it also turned her hunger off so hard she was barely eating. Some days she'd forget lunch entirely.

Her doctor flagged it. When you're eating that little, protein and muscle become a real concern, and just skipping food isn't a plan. So she went keto on purpose, focused on hitting protein first, and used the appetite drop as a chance to build better habits instead of just eating less of everything.

That's the pattern I keep hearing. The shot handles the wanting-to-eat part. Keto handles the what-do-I-eat part. They're not really competing. They're doing different jobs.

And this is exactly where knowing your numbers matters. If you're eating way less than usual, you want to make sure you're still getting enough protein to hold onto muscle. Our KetoDial calculator is handy for that, it gives you a protein floor to aim for so you're not just under-eating and hoping for the best.

Why plenty of people are choosing keto anyway

Let's be fair to the drugs. They work, and for some people they're life-changing. I'm not here to knock anyone who takes them. Do what works for your body and your doctor.

But GLP-1s aren't a free lunch either, and the community talks about this a lot.

  • Cost. Without insurance the sticker price is rough, and coverage is a coin flip.
  • Side effects. Nausea, gut stuff, and feeling wiped out are common complaints.
  • The forever question. A lot of people regain weight when they stop, which makes it feel less like a fix and more like a subscription.

So some folks look at all that and go, "I'd rather change how I eat." Keto gives them something they can steer themselves, no pharmacy involved. That's not stubbornness. That's a valid call.

My honest read on the whole thing

I'm not a doctor and I'm not the science person around here. But as someone who lives in these communities all day, here's what I actually think is happening.

Keto didn't die. It stopped being the only game in town. For years low-carb was the loud kid in the room, and now there's a new loud kid. That feels like a threat, but it's really just more options for people trying to solve the same problem.

The smart voices in the community already figured this out. They're not asking "keto OR Ozempic." They're asking "what combination fits my life, my budget, and my body." That's a better question.

The best take I saw all month: "The diet didn't fail you and the drug isn't magic. They're both just tools. Pick the one you'll actually stick with."

So if you're one of the nervous-middle people wondering if keto still counts, breathe. It counts. Nobody handed you a rule that says you had to quit because a new option showed up.

Eat in a way you can keep up with. Hit your protein. Know your numbers. And if you ever want a starting point for the macro side of things, the calculator will sort it out for you in about two minutes.

Keto's not over. It just has company now. And weirdly, that's made the community a lot kinder to each other.

One quick note: I'm not a doctor, and none of this is medical advice. If you're on a GLP-1 medication, have a health condition, or you're thinking about big changes to how you eat, talk to your healthcare provider first. They know your situation, I don't.
