Keto and Fatty Liver: The 31% Reversal Data

Sarah · Health Coach · July 24, 2026

The number that surprised me

When I first started reading about fatty liver, I expected the fix to be all about losing a lot of weight. Drop the pounds, drop the liver fat. Simple, right?

Then I found a study that flipped that assumption. Researchers put people on a ketogenic diet and saw their liver fat fall by about 31 percent in just six days. The wild part? Their body weight only dropped about 3 percent. The liver got dramatically better before the scale had much to say about it.

That gap between "barely lost weight" and "liver fat way down" is why I think NAFLD reversal is one of keto's most underrated wins. Let me walk you through what the data actually shows.

What the researchers found

The study I keep coming back to was published in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS) in 2020, led by Panu Luukkonen with Gerald Shulman of Yale and Hannele Yki-Järvinen of the University of Helsinki. It's a small, tightly controlled study. Ten overweight or obese adults ate a ketogenic diet for six days, and the team measured liver fat directly using MRI spectroscopy, which is a lot more precise than a standard scan.

Here's what they reported:

  • Liver fat (intrahepatic triglycerides) dropped by about 31 percent.
  • Hepatic insulin resistance dropped by about 58 percent.
  • Fasting insulin fell by roughly 53 percent.
  • All of this happened with only a 3 percent drop in body weight.

So the liver wasn't waiting on major weight loss. Something about the metabolic switch itself was doing the heavy lifting.

Why the liver responds so fast

Here's the part I find genuinely fascinating. On keto, your body shifts toward burning fat for fuel and making ketones. The researchers found that the fat being pulled out of the liver got redirected toward ketone production instead of being repackaged and stored as more liver fat.

In plain terms, the liver stopped hoarding fat and started shipping it out. Ketone production jumped sharply, and the liver's internal fat-building machinery slowed down.

The trigger for a lot of this was the drop in insulin. When insulin runs high, your liver gets the constant signal to store fat and hold onto it. Bring insulin down, and you flip that signal. That's why the researchers saw hepatic insulin resistance improve so much, and so quickly.

Why this matters if you have insulin resistance or PCOS

Fatty liver, insulin resistance, and PCOS tend to travel together. They share the same root problem, which is a body that's stopped responding well to insulin.

I'll be honest, this one is personal for me. When I was working through my own PCOS recovery, high insulin was the thread connecting almost everything I was struggling with. Fatty liver often rides along with PCOS, and a lot of women never get told about that connection. Studies have found NAFLD is notably more common in women with PCOS than in women without it.

So a way of eating that lowers insulin and clears liver fat at the same time isn't a small thing. It's addressing the shared cause instead of chasing each symptom on its own. When your liver is less fatty and more insulin sensitive, a lot of downstream stuff tends to get easier.

The honest caveats

I want to be straight with you, because overpromising helps no one.

That PNAS study was small. Ten people, six days. It shows a striking short-term effect and explains the mechanism beautifully, but it's not proof of long-term reversal on its own. It was also a lower-calorie diet, so calories played a role alongside the carb reduction.

The good news is it doesn't stand alone. Longer trials of low-carb and ketogenic eating have repeatedly shown meaningful reductions in liver fat, and the same research group has continued studying how ketogenic diets affect the liver in newer work. The direction of the evidence is consistent, even if the exact percentages shift from study to study.

What I'd take from all of it: keto looks like a genuinely strong tool for fatty liver, especially early NAFLD. It's not a magic eraser, and how much fat you're carrying, your genetics, and your consistency all matter.

How to actually put this to work

The bottom line

A ketogenic diet cut liver fat by about 31 percent and hepatic insulin resistance by more than half in under a week, with barely any weight loss. That tells me the benefit isn't just "you ate less." It's the metabolic shift itself.

For anyone dealing with insulin resistance or PCOS, that's a reason for real hope. Fatty liver often quietly improves right alongside everything else keto is helping with, and most people never even hear about it.

A quick note, because it matters: I'm not a doctor. I've researched this deeply and lived a lot of it, but I'm not your doctor. If you have a diagnosed liver condition, take medications, or have any medical concerns, talk with someone who knows your full picture before making big changes. Fatty liver can range from mild to serious, and it deserves real medical oversight. Everything here is educational, based on research and experience. Your situation might be different.
