Day 6 and Only Down 3 Pounds: Is Keto Working?

Sarah · Health Coach · September 29, 2026

You're six days in. You've cleared the pantry, figured out breakfast, and pushed through a couple of foggy afternoons. Then you step on the scale and you're down 3 pounds. Three. Your friend lost 9 her first week, and every article you read promised something close to that.

So is keto working? From what I've seen, it very likely is. Let me explain why 3 pounds is a normal number, and what to watch instead of the daily scale.

What the First Week Actually Takes Off

Most of what you lose in week one isn't fat. It's water and glycogen. Glycogen is the stored carbohydrate in your muscles and liver. Your body stores it with roughly three to four grams of water for every gram. When you drop to 20 to 30 g of net carbs, your body uses up that store, and the water goes with it.

That's why the typical first-week drop lands around 3 to 7 pounds. Smaller bodies and older bodies usually sit at the lower end. So 3 pounds isn't a failing grade. It's right inside the normal range.

Why Your Number Is Different From Hers

Where you land in that range has very little to do with whether you're "doing keto right." It mostly comes down to four things:

  • Starting size. A bigger body stores more glycogen and more water. Many people who post dramatic first-week drops started at a much higher weight.
  • How many carbs you ate before. If you were already eating fairly low carb, there was less glycogen to empty out.
  • Sodium. Salt changes how much water you hold. A salty dinner can nudge the scale up or down for a day or two.
  • Your cycle. If you still have one, the water you hold in the days before your period can hide a whole week of progress.

Smaller bodies also tend to lose slower the whole way through. I've heard from women who took six months to go from 155 to 135 pounds. That's steady, real fat loss, even though it looked nothing like anyone's highlight reel.

Medications matter too. Steroids like prednisone can cause big water shifts. Some people coming off a course drop 10 pounds or more in a couple of weeks. Most of that is water the medication put on. Then the scale goes flat for weeks and it feels like keto stopped working. If you take steroids or another medication that affects fluid, ask your doctor how it might show up on the scale.

The Real Signal Starts in Week Two

Once the water week is behind you, the scale starts telling you about fat. A sustainable pace is about 0.5 to 1.5 pounds a week. If you're over 50, 0.5 to 1 pound a week is a good pace, not a disappointing one. Half a pound a week, held steady, is real fat coming off, and it tends to stay off.

The first stretch isn't only about weight, either. Days 3 to 10 are usually the roughest, and most people feel adapted somewhere around weeks 4 to 6. Full fat-adaptation can take 8 to 12 weeks. For the bigger picture, here's a month-by-month keto results timeline.

What to Track Instead of the Daily Scale

A daily weigh-in mostly tells you about water. Here's what I'd track instead while you hold at 20 to 30 g net carbs:

  • Your net carbs. This is the number that matters most right now. If you're consistently at 20 to 30 g, you're doing the thing.
  • A weekly average. If you like the scale, weigh daily and only pay attention to the weekly average. Or weigh once a week, same morning, same conditions.
  • A tape measure. Measure your waist, hips, and one thigh every two weeks. Inches often move when the scale won't.
  • How your clothes fit. Some people see no scale change for a long stretch, then suddenly their jeans fit differently. The scale lags behind the body.
  • Hunger and energy. Fewer cravings, steadier afternoons, and not thinking about food all day are early signs your body is shifting.

You don't need to track calories yet. For the first 8 weeks, eat until you're satisfied. Keeping carbs low and meals simple does most of the work. If you're not sure what to put on the plate, these easy first-week keto meals keep it simple.

If you feel wiped out, headachy, or crampy, look at sodium first. Most people do better with 3 to 5 g of sodium a day on keto. One teaspoon of salt has about 2.3 g. If you've been told to limit sodium, check with your doctor first.

Give It a Fair Trial

One thing I've noticed over and over: frustration in week one is almost always about expectations, not results. Friends' stories set the bar, and patience is hard when you're finally doing something about it. That's human.

A fair trial is 60 to 90 days. That's when you can honestly judge whether keto is working for your body. A real stall is 3 to 4 weeks with no change on the scale or the tape measure. A slow week doesn't count. If you ever hit a real one, work through the keto stall checklist in order. Start with sleep, stress, and electrolytes. Next, do a 7-day food audit. Then recalculate your numbers at your current weight.

A Quick Word on Medications

If you take medication for diabetes or blood pressure, talk to your doctor before you cut carbs. Your needs can change fast, sometimes within days. Changes to thyroid medication are your prescriber's call too. Think of your doctor as a partner in this, not a hurdle.

Three pounds in six days is progress. Keep your carbs where they belong, pull out the tape measure, and give your body the weeks it needs to show you what it's doing.

I'm not a doctor. I've researched this deeply and worked with a lot of people, but I'm not your doctor. If you have health conditions, take medications, or have been told to limit sodium, talk to someone who knows your full medical picture before changing anything.
