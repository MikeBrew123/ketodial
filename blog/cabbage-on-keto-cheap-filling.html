Cabbage on Keto: The Cheap Vegetable That Fills You Up

Chloe · Community Manager · October 23, 2026

Okay, so here's something that happens to almost everyone a few weeks into keto. You open the fridge and there are two heads of cabbage in there, a bag of coleslaw mix, and half a wedge wrapped in plastic. You don't remember deciding to become a cabbage person. It just happened.

Real talk: this is one of the smartest things you can do on a budget. Cabbage is cheap and filling, it keeps for weeks, and it works in way more meals than you'd guess. Here's how many carbs it really has, how to store it, and five weeknight meals that use it.

How Many Carbs Are in Cabbage?

These numbers come from USDA data for raw green cabbage, rounded. They're close enough for daily tracking.

  • 1 cup raw, shredded: about 2 to 2.5 g net carbs
  • 1 cup raw, chopped: about 3 g net carbs (chopped packs tighter, so more cabbage fits in the cup)
  • 1 cup cooked: about 5 to 6 g net carbs

That cooked number surprises people, so here's the reason. Cabbage loses water and shrinks a lot when you cook it. It doesn't gain carbs, you're just fitting about twice as much into the same cup. If you roast a big tray and eat two cups, count it as cooked, not raw.

On a standard target of 20 to 30 g net carbs a day, a generous serving of cabbage fits easily. If you want to see exactly where it lands in your day, the KetoDial calculator gives you your daily carb, protein and fat targets so you're not guessing.

Why It Helps You Stay Full

Here's the thing about volume. A full plate looks and feels like a real meal, and a lot of people starting keto miss that most. A big pile of roasted cabbage next to your protein takes time to chew and fills the plate for very few carbs.

Let's keep it honest, though. Protein does most of the work of keeping you full. Cabbage helps by adding bulk. Build the meal around the meat, eggs or fish, then add cabbage to fill the plate.

And if you're new, don't count calories for the first 8 weeks. Eat until you're satisfied and let your appetite settle. If your weight stalls later, cut back the butter and oil you add to the pan first. Keep eating the same amount of protein.

What It Costs and How Long It Keeps

Prices vary a lot by region and season. Most of the time, green cabbage is one of the cheapest things in the produce section. Even in Whistler, where groceries can make you wince, a head usually costs less than a single bag of salad greens. A medium head gives you somewhere around 10 cups shredded, which is a lot of side dishes.

Storage is where cabbage really wins:

  • Whole head, in the crisper drawer: several weeks, often up to two months. Peel off a wilted outer leaf and it's fine underneath.
  • Cut head, wrapped tight: about a week.
  • Shredded or bagged slaw mix: 3 to 5 days, so use it first.

That's why it shows up in so many budget plans, including our $30 keto week. It's still good on day 12, long after the lettuce has gone slimy.

A Quick Word on Bloating

I'm not going to pretend cabbage is gentle on everyone. Some people get gassy from it, especially from big bowls of raw slaw. Start with a smaller portion and see how you feel. Cooked cabbage is often easier on the stomach than raw.

If bloating is a bigger issue for you, our post on what actually changes with keto bloating goes deeper. Also, you don't have to eat cabbage to "get your fiber." Low-carb vegetables are a normal, good part of keto, but they're optional.

5 Easy Weeknight Ways to Cook It

1. Roasted cabbage wedges. Cut a head into 8 wedges through the core so they hold together. Brush with olive oil or melted butter, salt and pepper to taste, and roast at 425°F for about 25 to 30 minutes, flipping once. The edges go brown and sweet-tasting. Serve next to chicken thighs or a pork chop.

2. Egg roll in a bowl. Brown a pound of ground pork, add garlic and ginger, then a bag of coleslaw mix and a splash of soy sauce. Stir for about 5 minutes until it softens. Finish with a little sesame oil and green onion. It's one pan and ready in 15 minutes.

3. Creamy cabbage with sausage. Slice smoked sausage and brown it, then add chopped cabbage and a bit of butter. Cook it down, then stir in a splash of heavy cream and let it thicken. Check the sausage label, because some brands add sugar.

4. Simple slaw. Shredded cabbage, mayo, a spoon of apple cider vinegar, salt and pepper. That's the whole recipe. Bottled slaw dressings are often loaded with sugar, so making your own is worth the two minutes. Go easy on portion size if raw cabbage bothers you.

5. Cabbage noodles. Slice cabbage into long, wide ribbons and sauté them in butter until just tender. Top with meat sauce or Alfredo-style chicken. It isn't pasta and won't fool anyone, but it holds a sauce really well. See how it stacks up in our keto pasta and pizza swaps, ranked. Jarred marinara can sneak in sugar too, so read the label.

Quick Comparison: Which One Tonight?

  • Roasted wedges: about 30 minutes, mostly hands-off. Best side dish for any protein.
  • Egg roll in a bowl: about 15 minutes, one pan. Best full meal on a busy night.
  • Creamy cabbage with sausage: about 20 minutes. Best comfort food, and it reheats well.
  • Simple slaw: 5 minutes, no cooking. Best for hot days and leftover meat.
  • Cabbage noodles: about 10 minutes. Best when you miss pasta night.

Raw carb counts, roughly, per cup: green about 2 to 3 g net, napa about 1.5 g, savoy about 2 g, red about 4 to 5 g. Red has a few more carbs but it looks great in a slaw.

So if you've suddenly turned into a cabbage person, you're in good company. It's cheap, it lasts, and it makes a keto plate feel like a real dinner. Grab a head this week and try the roasted wedges first. They're the one most people end up making again and again.
