Keto Without the App: 7 Rules That Replace Logging

Marcus · Performance Coach · September 15, 2026

You don't quit keto over a bagel. You quit at 9 PM on day 41, standing in the kitchen, weighing shredded cheese on a scale because the app won't let you close the day without it.

Two versions of this problem keep landing in my inbox. One person wants out of counting entirely and asks for food rules instead. The other wants an app so frictionless it survives a bad week. Both of them are asking the same thing: give me something that still works when life gets hard.

Here's the protocol. Run your numbers once. Convert them into seven rules. Then stop logging and let the rules do the work.

Rule 1: Run your numbers once, then close the calculator

You need three numbers, not a daily diary. Protein, net carbs, and a rough sense of your calorie ceiling. The KetoDial calculator gives you those in about two minutes.

Protein is 0.8 to 1.0 grams per pound of your goal body weight. Goal weight, not current weight. If you're 178 and heading for 150, that's 120 to 150 grams a day. Write it on an index card and put it on the fridge.

Rule 2: Anchor every meal with 30 to 40 grams of protein

This is the rule that replaces the most logging. Three meals at 40 grams gets you to 120. Three at 45 gets you to 135. You don't need a scale to hit it.

  • 4 to 5 oz of cooked meat, fish, or poultry: 30 to 40 g
  • 3 eggs plus 3 oz of sausage: about 33 g
  • One palm-sized portion, thickness included, is the visual shortcut

Spreading protein across meals beats one giant dinner dose, and that gap widens after 50. Your body handles 35 grams at 8 AM better than it handles an extra 35 at 7 PM.

Rule 3: Measure added fat. Nothing else.

Protein is the anchor. Fat is the lever. A satiety baseline of roughly 1:1 fat to protein by grams works for most people, so if you're eating 135 g of protein, you're somewhere near 135 g of fat.

Here's the part worth measuring: the fat you add, not the fat that came with the food. A tablespoon of butter is about 100 calories and 11 grams of fat. Oil is about 120. Three tablespoons a day is roughly 300 calories that never show up in your memory of the meal.

So use a spoon, not your wrist. One to two tablespoons per meal, poured deliberately. Ribeye and bacon still have calories, and that math catches up with people around month three. When you need to cut later, you trim added fat first and leave the protein alone.

Rule 4: Carbs get one plate rule, not a running total

Standard keto target is 20 to 30 grams of net carbs. You can hold that without an app if you make it a rule about what goes on the plate instead of a number you chase all day.

The rule: protein, above-ground vegetables, and added fat. No bread, no rice, no potato, no sugary drinks, nothing labeled "low carb" in a wrapper. Low-carb vegetables are fine and normal on keto. Two cups of leafy greens and a cup of broccoli lands you around 8 to 10 grams net, which leaves plenty of room.

You only count carbs when you introduce a new food. Check it once, learn it, and never check it again.

Rule 5: Build nine meals, not ninety

Three breakfasts, three lunches, three dinners. That's your rotation. Once a meal is built to hit 30 to 40 grams of protein with measured fat, it lands in range every single time you eat it, forever, with zero mental effort.

This is the whole trick. Logging is expensive because every meal is a new decision. Repeat meals are free. Yes, they get dull, and boring meals are a real reason people quit in week 6, so rotate one slot a month instead of rebuilding the system.

Rule 6: Don't count calories for the first eight weeks

Eat to satiety. Hit your protein, keep carbs in range, measure added fat, stop when you're full.

Some people find this hard to accept, because counting feels like control. But protein and fat do their own appetite regulation, and the first eight weeks are when you're learning what full actually feels like again. Counting calories on top of that is a second job you don't need yet.

Rule 7: Log one week a month, not every week

Here's the spot check. Pick one week a month. Log everything for seven days, including the butter and the cream in your coffee. Then stop.

That week tells you two things. Whether your rules are still landing where you think they are, and whether portion creep has crept. It takes seven days of effort per month instead of thirty.

Do it honestly. Food trackers, including the AI ones that guess from a photo, are unreliable on fat grams specifically, which is the exact number your audit exists to check. Weigh the added fat during audit week and let the app estimate the rest.

What "working" looks like

After the first week of water weight, 0.5 to 1.5 lb per week is real fat loss. Over 50, 0.5 to 1 lb per week is a good pace. Anything in that band means the rules are working and you should change nothing.

A real stall is 3 to 4 weeks with no movement on the scale and no movement on the tape measure. Two flat weeks is not a stall. When you hit a genuine one, the order is: check sleep, stress, and electrolytes first, then run your 7-day audit, then recalculate at your current weight.

That last step matters more than people expect, because the numbers you set at 178 lbs are wrong at 158. That's a different problem from this one, and it has its own fix: recalculate your macros every 10 pounds. This post is about not being able to sustain the logging. That one is about your target going stale underneath you.

Run this week

Monday: get your protein number from the calculator and write it down. Tuesday: build three breakfasts that each hit 35 grams. Wednesday through Sunday: eat them, measure your added fat with a spoon, and don't open an app.

Stop overthinking it. The system beats the diary because the system doesn't need you to be having a good day.
