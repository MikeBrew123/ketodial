Diet Soda on Keto: What an 18-Month Water Swap Found

Sarah · Health Coach · November 13, 2026

If you drink three or four diet sodas a day and the scale hasn't moved in a month, someone has probably told you the soda is the problem. Maybe that someone was you, at 3 pm, opening can number three.

A study in the news this month looks like it answers that question. It doesn't quite. But it does give you a cheap two-week experiment worth running, and I'll walk you through both.

Before you blame the soda, check the basics. Use the free KetoDial calculator to recheck your carb and protein targets at your current weight, because what worked 20 pounds ago may not fit now. Most people on keto aim for 20-30 g net carbs a day. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

What the study actually did

The research comes from Farshchi Nasr and colleagues. It was presented as a conference poster abstract (586-P) in the 2025 supplement of the journal Diabetes, and it reached the news in November 2026. That matters. It's an abstract, not a full peer-reviewed paper yet, so we're working from a summary.

About 80 women took part. All had overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes, and all regularly drank diet drinks. Both groups followed the same structured weight-management program: six months of weight loss, then twelve months of maintenance, 18 months in total. The reports don't say what kind of diet it was, so I won't guess.

Here's the swap. One group replaced their usual diet drink after lunch with water, five days a week. The other group kept drinking their diet drinks. That's one drink on weekdays. Not every can, and not a ban.

What they found

The water group lost an average of 6.82 kg. The diet-drink group lost 4.85 kg. That's about 2 kg more, or roughly 4 lb, over a year and a half.

The water group also showed bigger improvements in fasting blood sugar, insulin resistance and blood fats. The reports don't give figures for those, so I can't tell you how big the differences were.

Four pounds over 18 months isn't dramatic. But it came from changing one drink while everything else stayed the same. For that little effort, a measurable difference is worth paying attention to.

What it doesn't tell you

  • It wasn't a keto trial. We don't know what these women ate, so we can't say the result carries over to someone eating 20-30 g of carbs a day.
  • Everyone had type 2 diabetes. Many keto readers don't, and the effect could be different for them.
  • It's an abstract. Full papers often add detail that changes how a result reads.
  • Nobody knows why water won. This is the big one.

There are ideas. Maybe a sweet taste keeps cravings alive. Maybe the after-lunch can is a habit cue that pulls a snack along with it. Those are ideas, not proven.

The broader research is mixed too. A separate 2025 study by Chakravartti and colleagues in Nature Metabolism, with about 75 adults, found more reported hunger and changed activity in the brain's hunger center after sucralose than after sugar. But hunger after sucralose was no different from hunger after water. So "diet soda makes you hungry" isn't settled, and anyone who tells you it is has gone past the evidence.

First, is it really a stall?

A true stall is 3-4 weeks with no movement on the scale or the tape measure. One flat week isn't a stall. Neither is a scale that's stuck while your jeans fit looser.

It also helps to know what normal looks like. Sustainable fat loss usually runs about 0.5-1.5 lb a week, and after 50 it's often closer to 0.5-1 lb. Slower than you hoped isn't the same as stopped.

If it really is a stall, here's the order I'd go in. You can find all of it in the keto stall checklist:

  • Sleep, stress and electrolytes first. Short nights and a stressful month can stall anyone. If you're hungry in a way that feels new, week 3 hunger isn't always about electrolytes is worth a read.
  • Then a 7-day food audit. Write down everything, including the cream in your coffee and the "just a few" nuts.
  • Then recalculate your targets at your current weight.

The soda swap isn't a replacement for any of that. It's one cheap experiment that sits beside it.

The two-week soda test

Here's how I'd set it up if you drink several diet sodas a day and you've ruled out the basics.

  • Swap the sodas, not your food. Replace them with water, sparkling water or plain tea or coffee. Coffee is fine. If sleep is part of your stall, stop it after noon.
  • Keep everything else the same. Same meals, same carb target, same bedtime. Change one thing so you can tell what worked.
  • Measure more than the scale. Take your waist measurement on day 1 and day 14. Water weight bounces around; the tape is steadier.
  • Track cravings. Each evening, jot a quick note. Did you want something sweet after lunch? Did you go looking for a snack at 3 pm? Did evenings feel easier? If evenings are your hard time, night cravings on keto covers that in more depth.

If going cold turkey feels like too much, copy the study. Swap just the after-lunch can, five days a week. That's exactly what the trial tested, and it's a lot easier to stick with.

I'm a black-coffee person myself, so I won't pretend I know the pull of a cold can at 3 pm from the inside. But I know habits, and the hardest part of this test is usually the first four or five days, when your hand reaches for the fridge out of routine.

Reading your results

After two weeks, look at three things: the tape, the scale and your craving notes.

If your waist moved or your afternoons got quieter, you've learned something useful about your body. Keep going.

If nothing changed, that's useful too. Go back to your diet soda without a shred of guilt. The study showed a modest average effect in one group of women, not a rule for everyone. Plenty of people lose weight on keto with a can in hand, and drinking it doesn't mean you're doing anything wrong.

Either way, you'll have tested the question instead of arguing with yourself about it. And if the stall hangs on, run your numbers through the KetoDial calculator again and work back through the checklist.
