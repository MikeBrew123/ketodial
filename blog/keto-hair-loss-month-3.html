Keto Hair Loss Around Month 3. Here's the Fix

Chloe · Community Manager · September 18, 2026

First, the thing nobody tells you: it's not your imagination

You're three months in. The scale is finally moving, your jeans fit, you've stopped thinking about bread at 3pm. And then you look down at the shower drain and your stomach drops.

So let's not do the thing where I tell you it's fine before I've admitted what you're seeing. It's more hair. You're not being dramatic, and you're not imagining that your ponytail got thinner. Hair shedding on keto is one of the most common complaints in month three, and it comes up constantly.

Here's the part that actually helps: it's usually temporary, it has a name, and the two biggest triggers are both things you can change this week.

Your hair is reacting to something you did in week two

What most people are dealing with is telogen effluvium. Every hair on your head is either growing or resting. When your body gets a big shock, a batch of growing hairs quits early and flips into the resting phase. They don't fall out right away. They sit there for weeks, still attached, waiting.

Then the new growth pushes them out, all at once, in a wave.

That delay is the whole reason this is so confusing. The shedding you're seeing today was set in motion roughly two to three months ago, back when you slashed your carbs and your calories in the same week and felt fantastic about it. By the time your hair reacts, you've already forgotten the thing it's reacting to.

I want to be straight with you about that timeline. It's the pattern clinicians describe for this kind of shedding in general, not a number from a keto trial. There's no good study that says "keto hair loss peaks on day 90." What there is: a very consistent story from people who start hard, and a well-understood hair cycle that explains why the lag is what it is.

The two triggers you can actually control

Almost every case I'd put money on comes down to a combination of these two, not one or the other.

  • The deficit was too big. Rapid weight loss is a stressor. Your body triages, and hair is the first thing it deprioritizes, because you don't need it to survive.
  • Protein quietly dropped. This one sneaks up on people. Hair is mostly protein. When you cut carbs and start chasing fat, protein often ends up lower than it was before you started keto, and nobody notices because the scale is cooperating.

That second one is the real villain, and it's the one people fix last.

Fix one: get your protein right, and check the denominator

Our target is 0.8 to 1.0 grams of protein per pound of your goal body weight. Up to 1.2 if you're training hard. Goal weight, not current weight, and that distinction matters more than anything else in this post.

So if you're 195 pounds heading for 150, you're aiming for about 120 to 150 grams a day. Not 195. Not "whatever's in the eggs."

Then split it up. Thirty to forty grams per meal beats one enormous steak at dinner, and that gap widens after 50. Your body handles protein better in spaced doses than in one giant hit.

If you have no idea what you're currently eating, the KetoDial calculator will give you a protein number to aim at, and our guide to keto protein foods covers what to actually put on the plate to hit it. If you've dropped ten or more pounds since you set your targets, it's also worth recalculating your macros, because the numbers you set in week one aren't the numbers you need now.

One more thing, because it's where people go wrong. Protein is the anchor, fat is the lever. If you need to tighten things up, trim added fat first, the butter and the tallow and the extra oil. Never cut the protein. Cutting protein to save calories while your hair is shedding is the exact wrong move.

Fix two: slow down, on purpose

After the first week of water weight, a sustainable pace is 0.5 to 1.5 pounds a week. Over 50, somewhere in the 0.5 to 1 pound range is a good target.

Go pull your weigh-ins. If you've been dropping three pounds a week for two months, there's your trigger, and easing back into that range is the fix. Not quitting. Easing.

Normally we tell people not to count calories for the first eight weeks and just eat to satiety. Shedding hair while losing fast is one of the few good reasons to actually look at the numbers before then. Run a week of honest tracking and find out whether you're eating far less than you think. A lot of people on keto genuinely are, because the food is filling and the appetite suppression is real.

What's worth asking your doctor

Here's where I stop being useful and your doctor starts. Hair shedding has real medical causes that have nothing to do with keto: thyroid conditions, low iron, postpartum changes, PCOS, and side effects from medications you're already taking.

I can't tell you which one you have, and neither can anything you read online. What I can tell you is that this is a reasonable bloodwork conversation to ask for, and that the markers people commonly ask about include ferritin and iron studies, a full thyroid panel, a CBC, and vitamin D.

Ask. Don't self-diagnose, and please don't start, stop, or change a medication or a supplement dose based on an article. That's your prescriber's call, every time.

When it's not a keto problem at all

Some things are a see-your-doctor situation rather than a diet-troubleshooting situation. Go get looked at if you have:

  • Patchy loss or distinct bald spots, rather than even thinning all over
  • A part line that keeps widening with no sign of regrowth
  • Shedding that's still going strong past the six month mark
  • Hair loss with other symptoms like fatigue, feeling cold all the time, or cycle changes

Even thinning across your whole head is the telogen effluvium picture. Patches are not, and that's worth a professional look.

If you want the longer version of the regrowth timeline, our sister site covers why month three is the worst of the shedding. Same hair cycle, same fix, more detail on what the months after look like.

Month three is a terrible time to quit

Once the trigger is handled, the shedding phase ends and regrowth follows. It isn't instant, and nobody can give you an honest date. Watch for short new hairs standing up around your hairline and your part. That's your answer, and it usually shows up before the mirror gives you any credit.

For readers in their 40s and 50s, it's also worth reading what actually changes on keto in perimenopause, because hormone shifts and diet shifts happen to overlap in exactly this age range, and untangling the two is genuinely hard on your own.

Month three is when the work starts paying off. Our month by month keto results timeline shows why this stretch feels the way it does. Raise your protein, ease off the gas, get the bloodwork conversation on the calendar. Don't throw away the month it finally started working.

I'm not a doctor. I'm someone who spends her days reading research and talking to people doing this, and I can tell you what tends to work and what tends to go wrong. I can't tell you what's happening in your body. Many people find these adjustments help, but if something feels off, or if your shedding doesn't settle, go talk to someone who can actually examine you and run the labs. That conversation is always worth having.
