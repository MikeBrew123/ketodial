Keto Thanksgiving Plate: Net Carbs for Every Side Dish

Chloe · Community Manager · November 10, 2026

Okay, so here's the thing about Thanksgiving on keto. The turkey was never the problem. The problem is the eleven side dishes around it, each one a little scoop, and by the end your plate holds a full day of carbs before anyone has said grace.

We Canadians did our Thanksgiving back in October. My family's version means turkey next to pancit and rice, plus Tita Cora checking everyone's plate. But a lot of you are sitting down on November 26 to the full American spread. People are asking the same thing in every keto thread right now: what do the sides actually cost in carbs, and how do I get through dinner without it turning into a whole thing?

So this is a guide you can screenshot or print. It covers the day of the meal and nothing else. For the next morning, I wrote a separate post on how to restart after one off-plan night.

First, Know Your Own Number

Most people on keto aim for about 20 to 30 grams of net carbs a day. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

If you're not sure where you land, the free KetoDial calculator works out your daily carb, protein and fat numbers in a couple of minutes. Once you know your number, you can tell how much room one scoop of stuffing really takes up. At 20 grams a day, a single dinner roll uses up more than half of it.

Net Carbs for Every Classic Side

These are approximate numbers for a typical serving. They vary a lot by recipe, so treat them as a rough guide. Your aunt's stuffing might be very different from the one I'm picturing.

  • Roast turkey (4 oz): 0 g
  • Turkey gravy, flour-thickened (1/4 cup): about 3 to 4 g
  • Mashed potatoes (1 cup): about 30 to 35 g
  • Bread stuffing (1/2 cup): about 18 to 22 g
  • Sweet potato casserole with topping (1/2 cup): about 30 to 40 g
  • Green bean casserole (1/2 cup): about 8 to 10 g
  • Canned cranberry sauce (1/4 cup): about 25 g
  • Dinner roll (1 medium): about 13 to 15 g
  • Mac and cheese (1 cup): about 40 g
  • Corn (1/2 cup): about 14 g
  • Dry red or white wine (5 oz glass): about 3 to 4 g

Real talk: a "little bit of everything" plate can land somewhere around 150 grams. Nobody's judging that. But it helps to see it written down before you're holding the serving spoon.

The Low-Carb Versions

Here's the good news. Almost every classic side has a version that tastes like the holiday and fits a keto day. Same approximate rules apply.

  • Pan-drippings gravy, reduced or thickened with a pinch of xanthan gum (1/4 cup): about 1 g
  • Cauliflower mash with butter and cream (1 cup): about 4 to 6 g
  • Sausage, celery and mushroom stuffing, no bread (1/2 cup): about 3 to 5 g
  • Roasted butternut squash with butter (1/2 cup): about 8 g
  • Green beans with butter and toasted almonds (1/2 cup): about 3 to 4 g
  • Roasted Brussels sprouts with bacon (1 cup): about 5 to 6 g
  • Cauliflower mac and cheese (1 cup): about 6 to 8 g
  • Green salad with oil and vinegar: about 2 g
  • Deviled eggs (2 halves): about 1 g

Low-carb vegetables are completely normal on keto. Green beans, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower and salad are the backbone of a keto holiday plate. Fill up on them.

The Three-Step Plate Plan

This is the part I'd actually stick on the fridge. It's simple on purpose, because nobody wants to do math with Uncle Rick talking politics across the table.

Step 1: Turkey and Green Vegetables First

Protein is the anchor. Start with a real portion of turkey, dark meat and skin included if you like it, then add one or two green vegetables. Put the gravy on top, butter on the vegetables. Fat is the lever that makes the meal satisfying.

When half your plate is turkey and the rest is mostly green, you'll be less hungry for everything else. That's not willpower. It's just a full stomach doing its job.

Step 2: One Favorite Side, On Purpose

Pick the one dish you'd actually miss. Not the dinner rolls from a bag. The thing your mom only makes once a year. For some people it's the stuffing. For others it's the sweet potato casserole or a slice of pie later.

Take a real serving, sit down and enjoy it. A planned half cup of stuffing at about 20 grams is a choice you made. Five "tiny tastes" of everything can quietly add up to 120. The first one feels like a holiday. The second one feels like a blur.

And if you decide to skip the favorite this year and keep it fully keto? That's a fine choice too. Neither one makes you a better or worse person at the table.

Step 3: Bring One Dish You Know You Can Eat

This is my holiday rule and I've never broken it. I bring a big dish of something I can eat, every single time. That way there's always at least one safe option, and nobody has to cook around me or ask awkward questions.

For Thanksgiving, the easy wins are the cauliflower mash, the Brussels sprouts with bacon, or a plate of deviled eggs. They travel well, everyone eats them, and nobody asks why you brought them. Tita Nena still asks me for recipes after every family dinner. Tita Cora still says it's a phase, and she eats the dish anyway.

A Few Things People Ask Every Year

What about wine? A 5-ounce glass of dry red or white is around 3 to 4 grams. Sweet wines, ciders and mixed drinks run much higher, often 10 to 20 grams or more.

What if I'm still hungry for something sweet after dinner? This is really common after a big holiday meal. It's often more about habit and the long day than actual hunger. My post on keto night cravings explains what's going on and what people do about it.

What if the scale jumps on Friday? It probably will. Extra carbs and salt pull water into your body, and that shows up on the scale fast. That's water, not fat, and it usually settles within a few days of normal eating. If your weight still hasn't budged weeks later, the keto stall checklist is the place to start.

One Meal Is One Meal

Here's the vibe I want you walking in with. Thanksgiving is one dinner. Even if you go well past your number, one off-plan meal doesn't undo weeks of good days. You eat your next normal keto meal and keep going.

Fill your plate with turkey and green stuff, pick your one favorite on purpose, and bring a dish you trust. Then go enjoy the people, because honestly, that's the hard part anyway. If you want to double-check your numbers before the big day, the KetoDial calculator is free.

I'm not a doctor. I'm someone who's spent years reading every thread and sitting at a lot of family tables. These are the numbers and habits people on keto typically use, and you get to make your own calls about your plate.
