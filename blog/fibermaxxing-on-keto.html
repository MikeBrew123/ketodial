Fibermaxxing on Keto: Can You Hit 30g?

Chloe · Community Manager · August 2, 2026

Okay so my entire feed has been chia seeds for about three months now. Chia water in a mason jar. Chia pudding with the little tapioca-looking bubbles. Someone stirring psyllium husk into a glass and drinking it like it's normal. The word everyone's using is "fibermaxxing," which sounds like something a 19 year old made up, because it is, and now it's the biggest nutrition trend of 2026.

And if you're keto, you've probably had the same reaction I did. Cool, great, love that for them. Meanwhile the fiber conversation is all lentils, oats, and black beans, which are basically a full day of carbs in one bowl. So the whole trend just kind of floats past us.

Here's the thing though. I got curious enough to actually run the numbers, and 30 grams of fiber under 20 grams of net carbs is doable. It's just tighter than the TikToks make it look, and there's a chunk of this trend that's genuinely oversold.

Where does 30 grams even come from?

The 25 to 38 gram range you see everywhere comes from general population guidelines, and those guidelines were built mostly on research involving whole grains, legumes, and fruit. Big observational studies, standard Western eaters, the usual setup.

Which matters, because nobody ran those studies on people eating 20 grams of carbs a day. The 30 gram number isn't a law of biology. It's a target derived from populations that eat nothing like we do.

That doesn't make it useless. Fiber does real stuff. It feeds gut bacteria, it slows digestion, it helps a lot of people stay regular during the first few keto months when things get, let's say, unpredictable. I just want to be honest that "30g" got treated as a magic threshold by the internet when it started life as a rough recommendation.

The math actually works, barely

I sat down and built a day on paper. Here's roughly what it looked like:

  • Chia pudding, 2 tablespoons of chia: about 10g fiber, around 2g net carbs
  • Half an avocado: about 5g fiber, around 1g net
  • 2 cups of raw spinach cooked down: about 4g fiber, around 1g net
  • 1 cup of broccoli: about 5g fiber, around 6g net
  • 1 ounce of almonds: about 3.5g fiber, around 2.5g net
  • Half a cup of raspberries: about 4g fiber, around 3g net

That's roughly 31g of fiber and about 15g net carbs. So yes. It fits. But look at what it took. Nearly every food on that list is doing a job, and there's not a lot of room left over for the stuff you actually want to eat, like cheese or a sauce or a snack that isn't a seed.

This is where running your own numbers matters more than copying my day. The KetoDial calculator will tell you what your actual net carb ceiling is based on your body and your goals, and some people have way more room than 20g. If you're sitting at 30 or 40 net, fibermaxxing goes from tight to easy.

The foods doing the real work

Real talk, most keto fiber comes from about six ingredients and the rest is rounding error.

Chia and ground flax are the heavy hitters. Chia is roughly 10g fiber per 2 tablespoons with almost no digestible carbs. Flax is around 4g per 2 tablespoons and disappears into anything.

Psyllium husk is about 4 to 5g per tablespoon and is essentially all fiber. It's also the one people mess up most, because if you don't drink enough water with it, you will regret it. Ask me how the community knows.

Avocado is the best deal in produce. About 10g of fiber in a whole medium one for around 3g net.

Berries, specifically raspberries and blackberries, run around 7 to 8g of fiber per cup. They're the only fruit that really earns its spot.

Cruciferous vegetables and leafy greens fill in the rest. Broccoli, Brussels sprouts, spinach, artichoke if you're feeling fancy.

Notice what's missing. Cauliflower rice, cucumbers, and zucchini are lovely, but they're mostly water. You can eat a mountain and barely move the number.

Where the trend gets oversold

Three things bug me about how fibermaxxing gets sold online.

First, more is not automatically better. Going from 8g to 25g will probably change your life. Going from 30g to 50g mostly changes how much time you spend in the bathroom. The curve flattens and nobody making content wants to admit that.

Second, ramping up fast is a genuinely bad idea. Everyone posting their 40g days has been building for months. If you jump from your current intake to 30g overnight, you're going to be bloated and miserable and you'll blame keto. Add about 5g a week and drink more water than you think you need.

Third, and this is the one people won't say out loud, some people do worse with more fiber. If you've got IBS or you're sensitive to FODMAPs, piling on chia and cruciferous veg can make everything angrier. There's actually decent research on low fiber approaches helping certain gut issues. The community treats fiber as universally good and it just isn't.

What I'd actually do

Don't chase 30. Chase "more than I'm getting now," and track it honestly for a week first, because most people massively overestimate.

Then add one anchor food. A tablespoon of chia in your morning coffee routine, or half an avocado at lunch. That's 5 to 10g without restructuring your entire day.

Give it two weeks. If you feel better, add another anchor. If you feel worse, that's information too, and it's more useful than a number on an app.

Honestly, the version of this trend I like is the boring one. Not chugging psyllium sludge for the algorithm. Just noticing that most of us eating keto default to meat, cheese, eggs, and not much else, and that adding some actual plants back in tends to make people feel better. Fibermaxxing is a silly name for "eat some vegetables," but if the silly name gets people eating some vegetables, fine by me.

I'm not a doctor. I'm someone who reads way too many keto forums and likes running the numbers on stuff. If you've got a diagnosed gut condition, take prescriptions, or have a history of digestive issues, talk to your actual healthcare provider before overhauling your fiber intake. Internet articles are not medical supervision.
